## POST 01 - Monday 2 November
**Format:** Animated Reel, 9:16, 15-20 seconds  
**Franchise:** Designer, Decode This  
**Working title:** AI made UX work faster. Is it making it better?  
**Purpose:** Discovery + credibility. Start a useful conversation about quality, not AI hype.

### On-screen script
| Frame | Timing | On-screen copy |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 / Hook | 0-3s | AI made UX work faster. **Is it making it better?** |
| 2 | 3-6s | NN/g surveyed 521 UX practitioners. |
| 3 | 6-9s | 54% worry about AI output quality. |
| 4 | 9-12s | 50% worry about data privacy and security. |
| 5 | 12-16s | Faster drafts mean little if people spend longer checking and fixing them. |
| 6 / CTA | 16-20s | Measure better work, not just faster work. **Agree?** |

### Caption
AI is making parts of UX work faster. That part is real.

But faster output doesn't automatically mean better outcomes.

In its October 2026 survey of 521 UX practitioners, Nielsen Norman Group found that:
- 54% cited output quality as a barrier to AI adoption.
- 50% cited data privacy and security.
- 66% said they used at least one agentic AI tool.

The interesting bit? The challenge is shifting from generating something quickly to knowing whether it's good, safe and useful.

My take: don't measure AI adoption by how many screens or concepts it produces. Measure whether the work reaching users is better.

**Where has AI genuinely improved your design work - and where has it created more cleanup?**

*Source: Nielsen Norman Group, AI in UX: Work Is Faster, But Practitioners Have Mixed Feelings, 23 October 2026. Survey of 521 currently working UX practitioners; convenience sample, not a census of the profession.*

### CTA
Comment with one task where AI saves you meaningful time, and one where you still don't trust the output.

### Production notes
- Use MotionKit / Contrarian Statement.
- Keep statistics large and uncluttered.
- Put a small source footer on the final frame or in the caption.
- No stock AI robot imagery. Typography, simple number cards and restrained transitions.
- Music optional; message must work without sound.

## POST 02 - Wednesday 4 November
**Format:** 7-slide carousel, 4:5  
**Franchise:** AI x Design Lab  
**Working title:** Your design file is becoming context for AI agents.  
**Purpose:** Saves + shares. Translate a product shift into practical designer actions.

### Slide copy
**Slide 1 - Hook**  
Your design file is becoming context for AI agents.  
Not just a place to store screens.

**Slide 2 - The shift**  
A design file used to mainly communicate layouts, components and interactions to people.  
Now it increasingly needs to communicate intent, constraints and rules to AI tools too.

**Slide 3 - What's changing**  
Recent Figma updates point in this direction:
- Code-backed interactive layers on the canvas.
- Specs that bring written context onto the canvas.
- Guidelines and code sources that help agents follow real design systems.

**Slide 4 - What this means for designers**  
A beautiful screen is not enough.  
The system needs to explain:
- What this component is for.
- When to use it.
- What not to change.
- How states and edge cases should behave.

**Slide 5 - Five things to make explicit**  
1. Intent - what user problem are we solving?  
2. Constraints - what must remain true?  
3. Patterns - which components and behaviours are approved?  
4. States - what happens when things fail, load or change?  
5. Evaluation - how will we know the result is good?

**Slide 6 - A new working loop**  
Frame the problem → define guardrails → generate options → inspect behaviour → test edge cases → refine.  
Let the agent accelerate exploration. Keep design judgement in the loop.

**Slide 7 - Close / CTA**  
Can your design system guide an agent without you standing beside it?  
Save this as your next design-system check.

### Caption
The design file is changing jobs.

It still needs to help people understand the experience. But increasingly, it also needs to give AI tools enough context to work within the system rather than invent around it.

Figma's October 15 updates are a useful signal: code-backed prototypes, Specs for written context, and guidelines/code sources for agent-aware design systems.

The opportunity for designers isn't to write more documentation for its own sake. It's to make intent, constraints and quality expectations clear enough that both people and agents can use them.

**A practical test:** give someone (or an agent) a component library and ask them to build a new flow. Where do they get stuck? That's your missing context.

*Source: Figma product updates and Specs announcement, 15 October 2026.*

### CTA
Save this. Then audit one component in your design system against the five questions on slide 5.

### Production notes
- 7 slides, strong type hierarchy, one idea per slide.
- Use a simple visual progression: design file → context → guardrails → evaluation.
- Avoid Figma UI screenshots unless using properly attributed, permitted assets.
- Final slide should carry the save CTA.

## POST 03 - Friday 6 November
**Format:** Animated Reel, 9:16, 15-20 seconds  
**Franchise:** Designer, Decode This  
**Working title:** AI UX Designer roles are growing. What skills matter?  
**Purpose:** Discovery + career relevance for early-career designers.

### On-screen script
| Frame | Timing | On-screen copy |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 / Hook | 0-3s | AI UX Designer roles grew **145%**. What does that actually mean? |
| 2 | 3-6s | Autodesk's 2026 report tracked job listings across Design & Make industries. |
| 3 | 6-9s | The role is not just about prompting. |
| 4 | 9-12s | Design judgement. Technical fluency. Workflow thinking. |
| 5 | 12-16s | Communication, collaboration and leadership still matter. |
| 6 / CTA | 16-20s | Learn the tools. Build the judgement. **Show both in your portfolio.** |

### Caption
A role worth watching: AI UX Designer.

Autodesk's 2026 AI Jobs Report lists AI UX Designer as a new entry among the fastest-growing AI-related job titles in its Design and Make industries analysis, with 145% year-over-year growth.

A useful reality check: this is not a claim about all UX hiring, or a guarantee that every market is hiring for this title. It is a signal from a specific set of industries and job listings.

The same report puts design skills at the top of the skills sought for AI roles, alongside operations, technical, communication, leadership and collaboration skills.

So what should an aspiring AI-era product designer build?

1. Strong fundamentals in interaction and product design.
2. The ability to understand how AI systems behave and fail.
3. Prototyping and technical fluency.
4. Evaluation skills: test outputs, edge cases and trust.
5. Clear communication of decisions and trade-offs.

Don't just add "AI tools" to your resume. Show a real workflow you improved, what you evaluated, and what changed for the user.

*Source: Autodesk, 2026 AI Jobs Report, 13 July 2026. Scope: Design and Make industries.*

### CTA
What would you add to this skill list? Share one skill you're actively building.

### Production notes
- MotionKit / Contrarian Statement.
- Make 145% the visual hook, but show the report scope in small readable type.
- Avoid implying 145% growth across the entire UX job market.
- Keep skill phrases as kinetic typography, not a long list on one frame.

## POST 04 - Sunday 8 November
**Format:** 7-slide carousel, 4:5  
**Franchise:** Design Gym  
**Working title:** Your portfolio should prove how you think, not just what you made.  
**Purpose:** Saves + meaningful comments/DMs. Evergreen career utility.

### Slide copy
**Slide 1 - Hook**  
Your portfolio should prove how you think.  
Not just what you made.

**Slide 2 - Start with the problem**  
What was happening?  
Who was struggling?  
Why did this problem matter?

**Slide 3 - Show what you learned**  
What did you discover from users, data or the business context?  
What changed in your understanding?

**Slide 4 - Make your decisions visible**  
Show the options you explored.  
Explain what you chose, what you rejected and why.

**Slide 5 - Talk about constraints**  
Time, technology, business priorities, accessibility, dependencies.  
Good design happens within constraints, not outside them.

**Slide 6 - Show what changed**  
What shipped?  
What improved?  
What evidence do you have?  
What would you do differently?

**Slide 7 - The portfolio test**  
Can someone understand:
- Why this problem?
- Why this approach?
- Why this decision?
- What changed because of your work?

If not, your case study needs another edit.  
**Save this before your next portfolio review.**

### Caption
A portfolio is not a museum of polished screens.

It's evidence of how you approach ambiguity, make decisions and work with constraints.

A hiring manager should be able to understand your contribution without having to guess:
- What problem you were solving.
- What you learned.
- Which decisions you owned.
- What trade-offs you made.
- What changed after the work.

And please, don't invent impact metrics. If you don't have outcome data, explain what you validated, what shipped and what you learned. Honest evidence is stronger than impressive fiction.

**If you're rebuilding your portfolio, which part is hardest: telling the story, showing impact or explaining decisions?**

### CTA
Comment with the part of your case study you struggle to communicate. I'll use the responses to shape a future Design Clinic.

### Production notes
- 7 slides, large editorial headlines, concise supporting text.
- Use one consistent case-study visual metaphor, not generic portfolio mockups.
- Highlight the four questions on slide 7.
